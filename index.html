About

I'm Liam. I get to play with upcoming tech and turn it into things that feel good in your hands and on your face. Spatial computing, smart glasses, computer vision, machine learning: anything that gives me a new toy to figure out.

I co-founded ohi Studio. Ohi builds products for smart glasses and wearables. AI will live on your smart glasses and look at the world with you. We're at the moment where the hardware finally matches the ambition, and the software to make that genuinely useful and genuinely fun is still being written. We're writing it. Ohi combines playful interaction design with agentic AI to build apps, games and tools for wearables, and takes them from concept to deployment in-house. No handoffs, no agency layer. That keeps us fast and lets us move where the hardware moves.

Competition wins turned into contracts: prototype and store builds for Meta, Snap and Google, ports to new hardware, and behind-the-scenes work with partners who can't be named yet, across fitness, AI skills training, games and utility. I also consult with teams building in this space.

Most of what I ship has AI in the loop. Agentic coding to get from idea to working prototype in days, Gemini and on-device models for speech and vision, generative tools for art and sound. It lets a tiny studio move like a big one, and it means my time goes on the part that matters: whether the thing is actually fun.

Before that I did a PhD in Computer Science at the University of Lincoln, and taught game design, HCI, and games programming as an associate lecturer. My doctoral research was on playful technology for wheelchair users: interviews and surveys turned into design guidelines, then two adaptive exergames built around what wheelchair users actually want from play. That work ended up at CHI, the top human-computer interaction conference, and picked up a Best Paper award.

I like prototypes you can actually hold, problems that don't have one right answer, and the moment when a piece of tech crosses over from "neat" to "I want one." Always poking at what's next.
